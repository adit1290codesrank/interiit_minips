## 1. Frontier streaming / simultaneous S2ST systems, 2025–2026, and what each implies for this task

### Takeaway
The frontier has split into two families. (a) **Moshi-style full-duplex codec LMs** (Kyutai Hibiki, Hibiki-Zero; ByteDance Seed LiveInterpret 2.0 and Google's real-time S2ST are also duplex/streaming end-to-end) learn *when* to speak from time-synchronised training data, and increasingly tune latency with RL (GRPO). (b) **Speech-LLM "thinker–talker" systems** (Qwen3-LiveTranslate, SimulS2ST-Omni, SimulS2S-LLM, StreamUni, "All In Good Time") emit text and then speech tokens under a learned or test-time policy. None is open-weight *and* Hindi→English *and* streaming speech-out. This is consistent with Sarvam training its own base, and it means the report's "the dataset is the policy" thesis still holds for family (a).

### Cited Findings
- **Hibiki (Kyutai, Feb 2025, arXiv 2502.03382).** Fr→En. Built on Moshi multistream, it "produces text and audio tokens at a constant framerate of 12.5Hz … along with timestamped text translation". Training is synthetic: word-level contextual alignment (MADLAD) is applied "either by inserting silences or by synthesizing targets with a voice controlled, alignment-aware TTS". 2B (16 RVQ) and 1B (8 RVQ) variants exist [V-code] — [kyutai-labs/hibiki README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kyutai-labs/hibiki/main/README.md). Weights are reportedly CC-BY 4.0 [S] — [sourcepulse listing](https://www.sourcepulse.org/projects/2396528).
- **Hibiki-Zero (Kyutai, Feb 2026, arXiv 2602.11072, ICML 2026 oral).** Fr/Es/Pt/De→En, "a 3B-parameter model … 8 GB VRAM should work, 12 GB is safe" [V-code] — [hibiki-zero README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/hibiki-zero). Stage 1 is supervised on sentence-level aligned data, which gives "a model that translates but with high latency". Stage 2 is GRPO RL that "optimizes latency while retaining translation capability", with a BLEU-based reward exploiting sentence structure. It reportedly adapts to a new input language with "<1000h" of speech [S] — [arXiv 2602.11072](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11072v1), [ICML oral](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/oral/71089). Long-form claims: ">5pts of ASR BLEU, 20pts of speaker similarity and lower latency compared to Seamless". Short-form claims: +3 ASR-BLEU over Hibiki, "on par with Seamless" [S] — [arXiv 2602.11072](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11072v1). A later paper reportedly says it was trained on 160K h [S] (same search extract; the paper was not identified). Sources disagree on the benchmark size (15 h vs 45 h) and on the number of tasks (4 vs 5) [S] — [ICML poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/66094). Weights are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 [S] — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/hibiki-zero-3b-pytorch-bf16). **No Hindi.** I found no 2026 Kyutai release adding languages [S] (search returned only Feb-2025 coverage).
- **Seed LiveInterpret 2.0 (ByteDance, Jul 2025, arXiv 2507.17527).** Zh↔En only. It is "end-to-end" and built on a "full-duplex speech understanding and generation framework". It cuts cloned-speech latency "from nearly 10 seconds to a near-real-time 3 seconds", and human interpreters judged it ">70% correctness in complex scenarios". The model is closed [S] — [arXiv 2507.17527](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.17527v2), [Seed blog](https://seed.bytedance.com/en/blog/seed-liveinterpret-2-0-released-an-end-to-end-simultaneous-interpretation-model-featuring-ultra-high-accuracy-close-to-human-interpreters-low-latency-of-3-seconds-and-real-time-voice-cloning). Its RL stage for latency is from memory, not verified this session.
- **Google real-time S2ST (research blog, 19 Nov 2025).** An end-to-end streaming model in the speaker's voice with "about a two-second delay", trained on **time-synchronised data**. Slator says it powers Meet and Pixel 10. A heise interview instead describes specialised translation models plus Gemini speech generation and an AudioLM-style design, so the architecture is contested [S] — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/real-time-speech-to-speech-translation/), [Slator](https://tech.slator.com/google-reveals-secret-sauce-behind-ai-live-speech-translation/), [heise](https://heise.de/-11123168). **Gemini 3.5 Live Translate** (Jun 2026) is streaming S2S in 70+ languages through the Live API and is closed [S] — [multilingual.com](https://multilingual.com/google-launches-gemini-3-5-live-translate-real-time-speech-capabilities/).
- **Qwen3-LiveTranslate-Flash and successors (Alibaba).** About 60 input languages, voice output for a subset, ~2.8 s latency. A later "Qwen3.8-LiveTranslate" is reported at 2.3 s LAAL. Naming is inconsistent across sources, and I found no Hindi-specific number. API only [S] — [Alibaba Cloud blog](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/qwen3%E2%80%91livetranslate-real%E2%80%91time-multimodal-interpretation-%E2%80%94-see-it-hear-it-speak-it%EF%BC%81_602585), [hermes-ai](https://hermes-ai.net/news/alibaba-s-qwen3-8-livetranslate-cuts-speech-translation-lag-to-2-3-seconds/), [MindStudio hands-on](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/qwen3-8-livetranslate-hands-on/). The hands-on review says it struggles past single sentences.
- **SimulS2ST-Omni (CUHK-SZ/Amphion, Jul 2026, arXiv 2607.19810).** A thinker–talker speech LM with "joint text-code trajectory supervision". It reports ASR-BLEU parity with LiveInterpret 2.0 on RealSI and ACL60/60, using ~2k h of paired S2ST data, and claims to hold up with 90% less paired data [S] — [arXiv 2607.19810](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.19810).
- **SimulS2S-LLM (Apr 2025, arXiv 2504.15509).** Trains a speech LLM offline and uses a *test-time policy* for simultaneous inference. It predicts discrete speech tokens and uses a pretrained vocoder, evaluated on CVSS [S] — [arXiv 2504.15509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15509).
- **StreamUni (ICTNLP, Jul 2025, arXiv 2507.07803).** A StreamSpeech-lab successor that uses speech chain-of-thought for segmentation, policy and translation in one LSLM. Its output is **text**, not speech [S] — [arXiv 2507.07803](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.07803).
- **"All In Good Time" (Hussein et al., 24 Sep 2026, arXiv 2609.30416).** LLM-based simultaneous S2ST. It names the bottleneck as a shortage of "causally aligned training data with high cross-lingual speaker fidelity" [S, mirror only] — [prismix mirror](https://prismix.dev/news/67bf19cfe13d).
- **NaturalFlow (Jun 2026, arXiv 2606.13121).** Targets the "disruptive pauses" in simultaneous S2ST output and uses End Offset plus ASR-BLEU (Whisper-medium + SacreBLEU) [S] — [arXiv 2606.13121](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13121).
- **Indic.** I found no AI4Bharat streaming S2ST. The closest items are IndicSeamless (SeamlessM4T-v2 fine-tuned on BhasaAnuvaad, offline speech→**text**), the BhasaAnuvaad / IndicVoices-ST speech-translation data and Indic Parler-TTS [S] — [ai4bharat/indic-seamless](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-seamless), [IndicVoices-ST](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4bharat/IndicVoices-ST).
- **Hikari (arXiv 2603.11578, 2026).** End-to-end streaming speech→**text** for En→Ja (a divergent-order pair), with a WAIT token for read/write and no separate segmenter [S] — [arXiv 2603.11578](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11578).

### Inferences
- For this task the only *structurally* relevant open systems are Hibiki and Hibiki-Zero (same `moshi` stack, 80 ms Mimi frames, text + audio streams). The speech-LLM systems are useful as ideas (trajectory supervision, test-time policies, RL latency reward), not as likely bases.
- Hibiki-Zero's GRPO stage is the most transferable new idea. It is permitted because reward models are allowed during training (PS FAQ) [V-PS], and RL needs rollouts rather than extra records, so it does not violate G3. Only the pairs' targets must be Bulbul shubh, which applies to *supervised* pairs [V-PS]. The report mentions GRPO only in passing (Conclusion and notes, as a "stretch goal"). It deserves one line in B5/B6 as a post-reveal option.
- Google's emphasis on "time-synchronised data" and the "All In Good Time" framing of a "causally aligned data" shortage independently support the report's central claim that the timing rule in the training pairs sets the policy.

### Gaps
- I could not read primary pages for LiveInterpret's RL details, Seamless 2025–26 updates (none surfaced), or Meta/NVIDIA streaming S2ST. I found no 2025–26 Meta or NVIDIA streaming S2S release in search.
- Hindi support and Hindi latency for Qwen3-LiveTranslate and Gemini 3.5 Live Translate are unverified.

## 3. Hibiki-Zero's training data format, and adjudicating critique (a)

### Takeaway
**Critique (a) is wrong. The report's current wording is correct.** Hibiki-Zero keeps Moshi's **inner-monologue text stream**: padded target-text tokens, time-aligned to the target audio at 12.5 Hz. Placing those tokens requires per-word times *inside the English audio*. Its sentence-level silence insertion also needs to know where sentence and clause boundaries fall in the target audio. What Hibiki-Zero drops is **word-level source↔target (contextual) alignment**, not target-side word timestamps. The PS's English-timing mandate is therefore equally consistent with Hibiki, Hibiki-Zero, or any Sarvam model with a time-aligned text stream, and it proves nothing about which one the base is.

### Cited Findings
- **Code: Hibiki-Zero's runtime emits one text token per 80 ms frame alongside audio, with PAD and EPAD tokens.** `decode_and_send` asserts `tokens.shape[1] == lm_gen.lm_model.dep_q + 1` (text + audio codebooks). It reads `text_token = tokens[0, 0, 0]` and forwards it to the client unless it is in `(0, 3)`, the end-of-padding and padding ids. `2` is logged as "End Of Sequence token" [V-code] — [hibiki_zero/inference.py](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/hibiki-zero/blob/main/hibiki_zero/inference.py). Moshi's `LMModel` defaults to `existing_text_padding_id: int = 3` and `text_card = 32000` [V-code] — [moshi lm.py](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/moshi/blob/main/moshi/moshi/models/lm.py). Batch inference truncates output audio at the **text-stream EOS index** and drops pad tokens to recover the text, so text and audio share a frame clock [V-code] — [hibiki_zero/inference.py](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/hibiki-zero/blob/main/hibiki_zero/inference.py).
- **Code: Hibiki-Zero is built on the `moshi` library** (`from moshi.models import LMGen, LMModel, MimiModel, loaders`; depends on `moshi>=0.2.13`). Its CLI accepts `--lora-weight` and `--fuse-lora/--no-fuse-lora` [V-code] — [hibiki_zero/run.py](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/hibiki-zero/blob/main/hibiki_zero/run.py), [pyproject.toml](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/hibiki-zero/blob/main/pyproject.toml). So a LoRA-adapted Hibiki-Zero can be served directly.
- **Code: how a Moshi-family text stream is built from data.** moshi-finetune's `Interleaver` takes `Alignment = tuple[str, tuple[float, float], str]`, i.e. (word, (start, end), speaker). It converts these into a 12.5 Hz stream of word tokens, `text_padding` and `end_of_text_padding`, with an `audio_delay` text-vs-audio shift [V-code] — [interleaver.py](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/moshi-finetune/blob/main/finetune/data/interleaver.py). The `alignments` JSON is produced by whisper-timestamped in `annotate.py` [V-code] — [annotate.py](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/moshi-finetune/blob/main/annotate.py). moshi-finetune's last commit (2025-07-07) predates Hibiki-Zero, and its code has no `hibiki` references [V-code].
- **Paper (extract):** Hibiki-Zero follows Défossez et al. (2024) Inner Monologue, "a stream of padded text tokens whose content is the aligned text transcription of the audio being modeled". Sentence-level alignment uses punctuation: commas or colons "in a precomputed transcript of the target" guide where silences are inserted, with durations drawn from a uniform distribution scaled by a hyperparameter [S] — [arXiv 2602.11072](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11072v1). The paper "only requires sentence-level translation alignment easily obtained from punctuation" [S] (quoted in the report, line 161). The model card: "target speech and text tokens at a constant 12.5Hz framerate … along with timestamped text translation" [S] — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/hibiki-zero-3b-pytorch-bf16).
- **Where "drops word-level alignments" comes from.** Headlines say it "eliminates the need for word-level alignments entirely" [S] — [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/02/13/kyutai-releases-hibiki-zero-a3b-parameter-simultaneous-speech-to-speech-translation-model-using-grpo-reinforcement-learning-without-any-word-level-aligned-data/). In context this means *word-level aligned source/target data*: Hibiki v1's MADLAD contextual alignment, "Word-level matching … between source and target transcripts" [V-code] — [hibiki README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kyutai-labs/hibiki/main/README.md). Searches found Whisper word timestamps in Hibiki-Zero for **evaluation** (latency) and found no explicit statement naming the tool used for target-side training timestamps [S]. The data-preparation code is said to be forthcoming and was not in the repo at 0.0.4 [V-code: the repo contains only inference/server/frontend].

### Inferences
- **Verdict on critique (a), "the timing mandate PROVES Hibiki, not Hibiki-Zero": REJECT.**
  - The premise is false. Hibiki-Zero still has a target-aligned text stream (code-verified: per-frame text tokens with PAD and EPAD, EOS-indexed audio truncation), so it needs target-side word times like any Moshi-family model.
  - The inference is invalid even if the premise were true. Sarvam may have trained its own model and may simply require target word times for its own interleaver, data checks or evaluation.
  - The PS sentence "How you arrange the English against the Hindi is up to you" [V-PS] leaves the source↔target placement to the team. That is the part that differs between Hibiki (word-level contextual) and Hibiki-Zero (sentence-level + RL). It is therefore *neutral*, or if anything consistent with a recipe where the base does not hand you a word-level alignment.
- **Verdict on the report's A2 wording ("most likely Hibiki-family, not proven"): UPHOLD.** One refinement is worth adding as a footnote. The target-timing requirement narrows the base to "a model with a time-aligned target text stream (inner monologue)". That covers Moshi/Hibiki/Hibiki-Zero and plausibly an in-house Mimi model, and possibly a trajectory-supervised speech LM such as SimulS2ST-Omni. The 80 ms / 24 kHz framing is the stronger Mimi signal.
- **Practical consequence for the report: no change of plan.** English word timings are needed either way, and a neutral pair store keeping *both* word-level and sentence-level Hindi↔English maps covers both recipes. The report already says this in B5.
- **Risk in B5's rehearsal plan.** The report plans to fine-tune Hibiki-Zero 3B with moshi-finetune. moshi-finetune has no Hibiki or Hibiki-Zero support. It defaults to Moshiko, and Hibiki's `model_type == "hibiki"` uses conditioning tensors (`get_condition_tensors`) [V-code] — [moshi loaders.py / run_inference.py](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/moshi/blob/main/moshi/moshi/run_inference.py). Expect to patch the loader and conditioning. The "8–12 GB VRAM" figure is for *inference*, not training. Also, Hibiki-Zero's input languages exclude Hindi, so the rehearsal tests plumbing, not Hindi quality.

### Gaps
- The exact tool or procedure Hibiki-Zero used for target-side word timestamps in training data (whisper-timestamped? forced alignment?) is unverified because the paper was not readable and there is no public data-prep code.
- Whether the HF checkpoint's `model_type` is `hibiki` (with conditioning) was not checked, because the HF config was blocked.

## 4. Current best latency and quality evaluation practice for S2ST, compared with the report's metric set

### Takeaway
IWSLT moved from **StreamLAAL (2025)** to **LongYAAL (2026, Polák et al. 2025)** for long-form, unsegmented simultaneous evaluation. It reports both computation-unaware and computation-aware variants, uses the **Simulstream** toolkit, and pairs this with **xCOMET-XL / COMET + BLEU/chrF** for quality. For long-form S2ST specifically, the IWSLT 2026 practice is ASR + forced alignment of the output speech, then sentence-embedding re-segmentation, then **YAAL + xCOMET**. The report's set (AL, LAAL, StartOffset, EndOffset, ATD on the playback timeline, ASR-BLEU/chrF/COMET, entity and intent checks, UTMOS) is sound but one generation behind. It should add YAAL/LongYAAL and xCOMET, and a pause/naturalness metric.

### Cited Findings
- IWSLT 2025 simultaneous track: StreamLAAL for latency, BLEU plus neural metrics (COMET, BLEURT) for quality [S] — [MLLP-VRAIN IWSLT 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18828), [IWSLT 2025 findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.iwslt-1.44.pdf).
- IWSLT 2026: "Latency is measured using the non-computation-aware LongYAAL (Polák et al., 2025) on the development set, which is then used to assign systems to their respective latency regimes" [S] — [IWSLT 2026 findings](https://aclanthology.org/2026.iwslt-1.39.pdf). Participants report LongYAAL CU and CA with BLEU, chrF and XCOMET-XL on the MCIF dev set, simulated with Simulstream in a long-form unsegmented setup [S] — [AlignAtt4LLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03967), [MLLP-VRAIN 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.iwslt-1.24/), [Simulstream toolkit](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17648).
- Long-form S2ST evaluation (Xue, Ouyang, Li, CMU; IWSLT 2026, arXiv 2606.15059): ASR + forced alignment on the generated speech gives token timestamps. A sentence-embedding aligner then maps output to source sentences, yielding sentence-level **YAAL** and **xCOMET**. They find "current systems accumulate substantial latency on long speech" [S] — [ACL Anthology 2026.iwslt-1.3](https://aclanthology.org/2026.iwslt-1.3/), [arXiv 2606.15059](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15059).
- COMPASS (Sony/CMU, arXiv 2606.03241): 46 metrics across 8 dimensions. Systems differ by ">30%" on naturalness and speaker preservation but only "a few points" on translation quality. "Standalone MOS predictors did not predict listener preference" [S] — [arXiv 2606.03241](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03241).
- NaturalFlow: End Offset + ASR-BLEU (Whisper-medium + SacreBLEU) "following the IWSLT protocol", and targets disruptive pauses [S] — [arXiv 2606.13121](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13121).
- SimulEval's speech scorers implement AL/LAAL/DAL/ATD/StartOffset/EndOffset with `start = max(prev_end, delay)` [V-code, per prior notes] — [SimulEval](https://github.com/facebookresearch/SimulEval/blob/main/simuleval/evaluator/scorers/latency_scorer.py).

### Inferences
- The report's choice of SimulEval-style playback-timeline metrics remains the closest match to the PS's prose ("how far the English speech trails the speaker, and how soon it finishes after the speaker stops") [V-PS]. Sessions are 10–60 s, so long-form LongYAAL re-segmentation matters less than at IWSLT, but **YAAL** (or reporting LAAL *and* YAAL) would show the report is current.
- Using UTMOS for the audio KPI is weakly supported. COMPASS reports that standalone MOS predictors fail to predict listener preference. Add a pause metric (count or duration of internal silences > ~250 ms, à la NaturalFlow), because silence insertion is the main audio-quality risk.
- BLASER 2.0 is still a reference-free S2ST quality option. I found no 2025–26 evidence that it displaced xCOMET at IWSLT.

### Gaps
- Exact YAAL and LongYAAL formulas (Polák et al. 2025) and the 2026 S2ST-subtrack winners were not retrievable, since the findings PDF was blocked.

## 5. 2025–2026 work on simultaneous translation from verb-final/SOV languages into English

### Takeaway
I found no 2025–26 paper specific to Hindi→English simultaneous S2ST or Hindi verb anticipation. Current SOV-pair work is mostly En→Ja (Hikari's WAIT token; NAIST IWSLT 2025 Whisper + LLM) and LLM-era "human-like strategy" papers. The report's strategy is consistent with that literature, and no recent source contradicts it: monotonic/chunk-wise targets, holding verbs and negations rather than anticipating them, and offline references under-scoring simultaneous output.

### Cited Findings
- Hikari (2026): end-to-end streaming speech→text for En→Ja with a WAIT token, no separate segmenter [S] — [arXiv 2603.11578](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11578).

- STACL's patent text explicitly frames anticipation of sentence-final verbs for SOV→SVO [S] — [USPTO 11126800](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11126800).

### Inferences
- The report's claim that "anticipation is a liability under no-take-backs + intent/entity KPIs" is a task-specific argument (36/70 points on intent and entity [V-PS]).
