## Q1. Real Hindi / code-mixed speech corpora: licence, hours, style, verdict

### Takeaway
IndicVoices (CC-BY-4.0) is the main real source. It is mostly extempore (76%) and conversational (15%) speech, and it ships Level-1 verbatim transcripts, the exact convention the PS requires. Kathbath (CC0, 150 h Hindi, read), FLEURS (CC-BY-4.0, read, with parallel English) and MUCS Hi-En (CC BY-SA 4.0, about 90 h of code-switched technical lectures) are useful, clearly redistributable additions. Gram Vaani (academic-only or NC), the IndicTTS licence (conflicting) and Shrutilipi (broadcast audio of unclear provenance) should be left out unless the organisers clear them. Rasa has no Hindi at all.

### Inferences
- Only IndicVoices combines a redistributable licence, conversational/extempore style, many speakers and native **Level-1 verbatim** transcripts (the PS's own reference convention). It should dominate the real share. The PS also requires English words in Devanagari and numbers as words. IV-L1 probably already does this because the PS borrows IV's convention, but each file needs a check.
- IndicVoices-R and IndicVoices share source recordings. Under "union of the source-file intervals" they count once. Using the enhanced IV-R audio does not add hours, but it may count as a non-original "real recording". Prefer the raw IV audio as the source and treat enhancement as optional processing.
- Telephone conditions are best simulated with codec/bandpass augmentation, which adds no hours, rather than by importing Gram Vaani with its restrictive licence.
- ShareAlike (MUCS) and attribution (IV, FLEURS, IV-R) obligations should go in `data_card.md`. Mixing CC BY-SA content means that subset must stay under CC BY-SA.

### Gaps
- **Hindi-only hours of IndicVoices** (transcribed, per split, extempore vs conversational) could not be read, because the HF card and the explorer were blocked. The 300 h figure is a third-party aggregate.
- I could not confirm whether IndicVoices conversational files are single-channel two-speaker recordings (which would breach "one speaker per clip") or one side only.
- Native sample rates of IndicVoices and FLEURS, and the current Common Voice Hindi hours, were not confirmed.
- No licence text was found for Lahaja, Svarah, SPRING-INX or IndicTTS.

## Q2. Hindi↔English speech-translation and parallel speech corpora

### Takeaway
No public Hindi→English **speech-to-speech** corpus with conversational, code-mixed Hindi turned up. BhasaAnuvaad (CC BY 4.0) gives Hindi speech with English text, but its Hindi parts are lectures (NPTEL), one prime-ministerial speaker (Mann Ki Baat) and tutorials. That makes it a weak fit for a conversational, entity-heavy test, and much of the English text is mined rather than written as verbatim translation. CoSTA offers a Hindi-English code-switched ST *evaluation* benchmark with an unknown licence.

Bulbul v3 is technically strong but is a Sarvam API, whose outputs the brief says may not be redistributed.

### Inferences
- The two biggest licence traps are **NC weights** (XTTS-v2, F5 base, MMS) and **use-of-output clauses** (Fish's anti-training clause; Sarvam's "competing models" ban). The deliverable is shared with a company and used to train a speech-generating model, so both kinds of clause bite.
- For entity-heavy content (lakh/crore, account digits, dates), every TTS needs **text normalisation done by the team** before synthesis. The PS already demands numbers as words in the Hindi transcript, so the transcript and the TTS input can be the same string. An ASR round-trip check (e.g., IndicConformer, Whisper fine-tunes) filters skips, repeats and mispronunciations, as the PS requires.
- Voice diversity: Parler's prompted voices plus Veena's 4 voices plus IndicF5 with permissioned prompts give perhaps 20–50 distinct voices. That is far fewer than IndicVoices' thousands of real speakers, which is a further reason to keep real speech the majority.

### Gaps
- No primary benchmark was found on **Hinglish code-mix or number pronunciation** for any of these TTS systems. Veena alone claims code-mix support, and no published evaluation backs it.
- I did not confirm whether IndicF5's or Parler's training licences (e.g., IndicTTS's own licence) pass restrictions on to their outputs.

## Q4. Text sources for synthetic Hindi scripts (ideally with parallel English)

### Takeaway
**BPCC (IndicTrans2)** gives CC0 or CC-BY-4.0 Hindi–English parallel text, including **BPCC-H-Daily**, 139K human-translated everyday sentences. It is the best licence-clean seed. **PHINC** (CC BY 4.0, 13,738 human Hinglish→English pairs) and **Hinglish-TOP** (10K human + 170K generated code-switched queries *with English parallels*; licence unverified) add real code-mixing patterns. **L3Cube-HingCorpus is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, so avoid it. Entity-dense content (amounts, account numbers, self-corrections) must mostly be **LLM-generated** from templates.

## Q5. Evidence on mixing real and synthetic speech

### Takeaway
No universal optimal ratio exists. The evidence agrees that **synthetic-only training is clearly worse than real**, that **synthetic as a complement helps**, with roughly 1:1 to 1:3 synthetic-to-real a common sweet spot, and that **speaker diversity matters more than synthetic volume**. Hibiki, the closest analogue (streaming S2ST), relied on about 40k h of synthetic parallel data because real FR-EN parallel speech does not exist. Its synthetic data was resynthesised from *real* source-language audio transcripts.

### Cited Findings
- On LibriSpeech, a 50/50 natural/synthetic sampling ratio "edged out natural-only", 33/66 was "roughly comparable", and synthetic-only was far worse (about 50% WER on dev-clean). — [arXiv 1811.00707](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00707)
- 200 h of random-speaker TTS alone underperforms the real-only model, so synthetic data works best as a complement. Joint TTS/VC attribute augmentation cut Conformer-Transducer WER by 11% relative on Common Voice and up to 35% on LibriSpeech, but "naïve mixing… often underperforms due to mismatched distributions". — [arXiv 2503.08954](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.08954)
- For code-switching ASR, the best fine-tuning volume for Whisper-small was 2–3× the real data, and "the crucial ingredient is speaker diversity". — [arXiv 2601.00935](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00935v1)
- The first 20% of synthetic data (relative to real) gave the largest WER jump, with diminishing returns up to 100%. — [arXiv 2602.08696](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.08696)
- Hibiki made synthetic parallel data "by translating and resynthesizing the transcript of single-language audio". It used MADLAD-based "contextual alignment" ("a word should only appear in the target once it's predictable from the source") applied by silence insertion or alignment-aware TTS. The model card reports about 40k h of synthetic FR-EN plus a 900 h fine-tuning mix (resynthesised CVSS-T plus synthetic long-form). Models are CC-BY 4.0. — [Hibiki README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/hibiki); [arXiv 2502.03382](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03382v1); [Kyutai blog](https://kyutai.org/2025/02/10/hibiki.html)
- Fine-tuning on IndicTTS plus IndicVoices-R improved zero-shot speaker generalisation over IndicTTS alone, so diverse real speakers help even TTS. — [IV-R README](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/IndicVoices-R)

### Inferences
- The base checkpoint Sarvam supplies is already "trained on synthetic data" (PS §3 Part B). The marginal value of the team's dataset is therefore highest in **real, noisy, conversational Hindi**, the domain the base model has seen least. That argues for a real-majority mix.
- Hibiki's alignment trick applies to the *target* side. For this PS, the analogue is choosing a monotone, chunk-aligned English translation per Hindi sentence, using the Hindi word timings.

### Gaps
- No study was found on real/synthetic ratios for **speech-to-speech translation fine-tuning** or for Hindi specifically.
- I did not check SeamlessAlign, StreamSpeech or Synth4Kws.

## Q6. Recommended 200-hour budget, hour-counting tactics, misleading claims, open question

### Takeaway
Recommended mix: about **125 h real (≈62%)** + **about 65 h synthetic (≈33%)** + **about 10 h held back** for regeneration and filtering. Real audio is mostly IndicVoices extempore with L1 transcripts. Synthetic audio is entity-dense, code-mixed, LLM-written scripts voiced by permissively licensed open TTS. Telephone and noise conditions are covered by augmentation, which costs zero hours.

### Cited Findings
- Hour rules: real = union of source-file intervals ("Overlapping or repeated cuts of the same recording count once"); synthetic = "every distinct generated file counts, including the same text rendered in another voice"; augmentation "adds no hours"; clips ≤60 s; one speaker per clip; real recordings "only from datasets whose licence allows redistribution". — PS §3 Part A (`/home/user/interiit_minips/Sarvam.pdf`)
- IndicVoices: 76% extempore with Level-1 verbatim transcripts. — [GitHub](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/IndicVoices); [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.01926v1)
- MUCS Hi-En: 89.86 h, CC BY-SA 4.0. Kathbath: 150.2 h Hindi, CC0. — [OpenSLR 104](https://openslr.org/104/); [IndicSUPERB](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/IndicSUPERB)

**Proposed split**

| Bucket | Hours | Source / licence | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 Real extempore + single-speaker conversational | **105** | IndicVoices Hindi, CC-BY-4.0 | Matches the test (real, spontaneous, many speakers); native L1 verbatim transcripts; disfluencies and self-corrections occur naturally |
| R2 Real code-mixed | **12** | MUCS 2021 Hi-En, CC BY-SA 4.0 | Real intra-sentential English; keep it small because the technical domain is narrow |
| R3 Real read, with free parallel English | **5** | FLEURS hi_in, CC-BY-4.0 (plus optional Kathbath, CC0) | Human English references; acoustic and speaker diversity |
| R4 Real (optional) | **3** | Common Voice hi (CC0) or SPRING-INX if its licence is confirmed | Extra speakers |
| S1 Synthetic entity-dense code-mixed scripts | **50** | Open-weight-LLM scripts seeded by BPCC-H-Daily (CC-BY-4.0) and PHINC (CC BY 4.0), voiced by Indic Parler-TTS, Veena, IndicF5 and Svara | Covers lakh/crore, phone/account numbers, dates, negations, conditions, self-corrections, which are rare in real data |
| S2 Synthetic monologues with natural disfluencies | **15** | LLM scripts with inserted fillers/repetitions ("मतलब", "वो… वो") | Mirrors the verbatim-transcript style; teaches the model to ignore fillers |
| Reserve | **10** | — | Headroom for regenerating failed TTS files without breaching 200 h |
| **Total** | **200** | | **≈62% real / 33% synthetic / 5% reserve** |

### Inferences
- **Hour-saving tactics.** (a) Trim each real clip to its speech span (with VAD) before recording `source.start`/`end`. Silence inside the interval counts. (b) Cutting several overlapping training windows from one IndicVoices file costs nothing extra, so use generous re-segmentation for streaming practice. (c) For synthetic data, **give each voice a different text** rather than re-voicing the same text, because re-voicing doubles hours with little new content. (d) Do noise, telephone codec (8 kHz bandpass, μ-law/AMR), reverb and speed perturbation as training-time augmentation only; it is free and closes the telephone gap. (e) Do not take IndicVoices-R and IndicVoices clips from the same source file and assume they add hours. They count once, and IV-R's enhancement may raise "is this still the real recording?" questions.
- **Why a real majority.** The hidden test is real speech, and the base checkpoint is already synthetic-trained. Literature shows synthetic data helps as a complement up to about 1:1 but hurts when it dominates or lacks speaker diversity (Q5). The available permissive TTS offers only tens of voices against thousands of real IndicVoices speakers.
- **Why about 65 h synthetic.** Entity accuracy (18 points) and intent accuracy (18 points) depend on rare patterns that real corpora under-represent. Synthetic data is the only controllable way to add thousands of lakh/crore amounts, account digits and self-corrections with exact references.
- **Fallback.** If IndicVoices Hindi has fewer usable single-speaker extempore hours than 105, move the shortfall to Kathbath (read, CC0) and keep synthetic at or below about 45% of the total.

**Claims that turned out wrong or misleading when checked**
1. *Rasa as a Hindi source* (from the research brief): Rasa covers only Assamese, Bengali and Tamil. — [Rasa README](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/Rasa)
2. *IndicVoices size*: secondary sources and blogs quote 7,348 h (paper v1). The GitHub README says 12,000 h and the HF card 23.7K h. All are "total collected"; the transcribed share is far smaller (1,639 h, 3,200 h and 11.2K h respectively). — [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.01926v1); [README](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/IndicVoices); [HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4bharat/IndicVoices)
3. *Indic Parler-TTS licence*: AIKosh lists **MIT**, but the model card states **Apache 2.0**. — [AIKosh](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/home/models/details/ai4bharat_indic_parler_tts_text_to_speech_model.html); [card](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-parler-tts-pretrained)
4. *Gram Vaani licence*: AIKosh says **CC BY-NC 4.0**; OpenSLR says "free for academic purposes, commercial use by permission". Neither is a clean redistribution grant. — [OpenSLR 118](https://openslr.org/118/); [AIKosh](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/home/datasets/details/gram_vaani_hindi_asr_dataset.html)
5. *IndicCorp licence*: "CC BY-NC 4.0" (old page) versus "CC-0" (v2 HF README). — [IndicCorp page](https://ai4bharat.iitm.ac.in/indiccorp); [IndicCorpV2](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4bharat/IndicCorpV2/blob/main/README.md)
6. *"Vistaar is MIT"*: true only for the fine-tuned models. The bundled benchmark data keeps its source licences. — [Vistaar README](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/vistaar)
7. A search-engine summary gave Common Voice Hindi as "17 hours". That figure comes from an undated, clearly old blog post and must not be cited as current. — [CIS blog](https://voice.cis-india.org/common-voice.html)
8. *"IndicTTS has licence restrictions"* (from the brief): **neither confirmed nor refuted**. AIKosh and a 2024 paper point to CC BY 4.0, while the IITM download page binds users to a separate "License For Use of Indic TTS" whose text I could not retrieve. — [IITM](https://www.iitm.ac.in/donlab/indictts/database); [AIKosh](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/home/datasets/details/hindi_female_mono_indictts_phase3.html)

### Gaps
